My research interests include causal inference, statistical theory, and applied health research. During my PhD, I studied kernel-based methods for complex models, including gradient-free goodness-of-fit testing and weighted Stein discrepancies. My current work includes statistical modelling, model evaluation, and collaboration on healthcare research.

Selected research

STATISTICAL METHODOLOGY · PHD RESEARCH

Kernel and Stein methods for complex models

Developed gradient-free kernel conditional Stein discrepancy methods for goodness-of-fit testing and investigated measurement error through theory and simulation. Related research also examines weighted Stein discrepancies for Bayesian inference.

Methods: kernel methods, statistical inference, simulation, Bayesian inference

HEALTH ECONOMICS · OHRI

Oncology survival and decision modelling

Contribute to survival modelling and health economic research in oncology, including work on treatment sequencing, model inputs, and uncertainty in collaboration with research teams.

Methods: survival analysis, health economic modelling, sensitivity analysis, R

HEALTH DATA · OHRI

Clinical data quality and hospital outcomes

Work with clinical and administrative healthcare data, including sepsis-related data quality and discrepancy investigations, and apply statistical methods to hospital outcomes research.

Methods: healthcare data analysis, data quality assessment, interrupted time series
